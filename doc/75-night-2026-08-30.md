# Where the targets go: a phase-by-phase loss ledger, and what V1 does differently

Two nights of work. The headline is a measured loss ledger against DIA-NN's
33,330 confident S08 precursors, and the retirement of the hypothesis that
looked strongest going in.

## 1. The ledger

Peak identity is boundary IoU >= 0.5 against DIA-NN's own `RT.Start`/`RT.Stop`,
the criterion doc/71 adopted after +/-10 s was shown to carry a 10-30% decoy
null. The null measured for THIS criterion is 4.9%. Precursors are aggregated by
max DScore, never by min QValue -- doing the latter manufactured a 17% error rate
here where the truth was 2.4%.

    33,330  DIA-NN q<=0.01 on S08
      -646  NOT a loss: the frozen DIA-NN run searched CAM-free, all 646 contain
            cysteine and all 646 are in the library under (Carbamidomethyl)
      -170  fragment floor
    32,514  enter extraction
    -9,944  GATE C                  30.6% of what enters
    22,570  reach the picker
    -6,028  GENERATION              26.7%, against a 4.9% decoy null
    16,542  a correct candidate exists
      -758  RANKING                 4.6%
    15,784  the top-DScore candidate is the right peak
    -3,967  ADMISSION               25.1% of top-correct
    11,817  identified at q<=0.01 AT THE RIGHT PEAK
    +2,653  identified at q<=0.01 at the WRONG peak
    14,470  DIA-NN-confident precursors ODIA identifies (43.4%)

**Gate C is the largest single term and it runs BEFORE the picker**
(`PeakGroupScorer.cpp:1377` returns first), so 67.5% of the library never reaches
candidate generation at all.

### A correction this forces

An earlier finding here was "candidate generation is cleared at 99.94% proposal
recall, flat across every mobility-error stratum". That was measured on a
consensus set whose members had ALREADY passed Gate C. On the survivors,
generation is indeed near-perfect. Across everything DIA-NN identifies it is the
SECOND-LARGEST loss term at 6,028. The measurement was right and the verdict
drawn from it was wrong.

## 2. What was tested and refuted

**Narrowing the fragment m/z window.** This was the one difference from ODIA V1
that carried an argument, and V1's source states it verbatim
(`odiav1/src/opendialyzer.cpp:2955`): *"OpenDIAlyzer extracts at the configured
10 ppm -- six times wider than the instrument's real error, admitting
interference into every correlation sub-score."* V2's own full run agrees, at
`e2e_s08.log:170`: 15,602 accepted precursors, sigma 1.47 ppm, suggested
half-width 4.40 ppm, not truncated.

Measured on S08/mix10k at matched entrapment FDP, centre pinned so the sweep is
single-factor:

    window     FDP<=1%   FDP<=5%   FDP<=10%
    10 ppm       3,016     3,479      3,692
     6 ppm       2,981     3,304      3,627
     5 ppm       2,622     3,337      3,503
     4 ppm       2,767     3,186      3,427
     3 ppm       1,836     2,945      3,137

REFUTED, monotonically. The mechanism is the interesting part: narrowing
10 -> 6 ppm moved the candidate population by **three peak groups out of
51,515**. With sigma 1.47 ppm, +/-6 ppm is already 4 sigma -- essentially all
signal is inside it and so is the interference, which is co-eluting and
near-isobaric rather than spread across the window. Narrowing strips fragment
evidence out of the correlation sub-scores and removes almost no interference.

Also measured and negative, and not to be retried: raising the candidate cap
3 -> 10 (+52 proposals, +4 correct identifications, entrapment FDP 1.29% ->
2.94%); relaxing Gate C (doc/54, -20.4% at DIA-NN's operating point); `-use_pi0`
(its extra identifications are ~24% false); and the GBT parameter axis, which is
exhausted -- ODIA's booster is ~8.5% behind XGBoost at matched hyperparameters
and no exposed flag reaches it.

## 3. What was tested and SUPPORTED

**The retention-time window.** Same shape of lever, the other axis, and here
there is real dynamic range: 300 s is 21% of a 1,396 s gradient where +/-10 ppm
against a 1.47 ppm sigma is 7 sigma of nothing.

    RT window   FDP<=1%   FDP<=10%
      300 s       3,016      3,692     <- inherited, never measured
      150 s       3,157      3,971
      110 s       3,065      3,784
       60 s       2,674      3,761

+141 at FDP<=1% against a 99-target jitter band, and the curve TURNS on both
sides -- the pre-registered guard against simply shrinking the search space until
the number improves. Peak memory is flat at this library size (5,940 -> 5,954 MB),
unlike doc/54's Astral case where the same lever cut memory 4x.

I had been supplying 300 s to every arm in this campaign, inherited from an older
script and never measured, while the full-library run sizes itself to 110 s.

### The same lever at full scale, by accident

`samelib2` (ODIA on DIA-NN's own library) collapsed in pass 2: 14,709 -> 4,168.
The cause is the same axis, at a much larger magnitude:

    arm        pass-2 window   candidates scored   per precursor   pass-2 IDs
    samelib2       242.8 s        22,330,718           2.25            4,168
    e2e_s08        110.3 s         5,460,026           0.59           16,967

A 2.2x wider window admitted 4x the candidates, enriching the decoy null and
collapsing the count at fixed q. The window is not set directly -- it is
auto-sized from the RT map's refit residual, and `e2e_s08` used `-rt_seed cirt`
(p95 26.3 s) where `samelib2` used a map I fitted from DIA-NN's report
(p95 65.2 s).

So the chain is: **RT map quality -> window width -> candidate count -> decoy
null -> threshold**, and it is worth 3.5x at full scale. That is the same axis
doc/54 identified as this project's largest measured gain, now with a mechanism.

It also means `samelib2` cannot answer the library question it was run for: it
differs from `e2e_s08` in library, map AND window, and the window term dominates.
That is my error -- I supplied the map. `samelib3` repeats it with the window
pinned to 110 s.
